ABSTRACT
Rare-disease variants described in case reports often never reach ClinVar or
OMIM. GP-RareLink asks whether an LLM-based systematic-review method
(SLIDERS), run with Claude, can turn case reports into variant–phenotype–
disease records that a person can check value by value, at a demonstrable ac-
curacy. This interim report establishes provenance and feasibility; it does not yet
establish accuracy. Claude Opus writes per-person records in which every value
carries the PMID and a verbatim quote and phenotypes are three-state; a second
Claude read (the gatekeeper) removes claims their quote does not support; Clin-
Var and OMIM are held out of extraction and used only to score it; plan and
pipeline were frozen on 2026-10-20. The only human audits so far, two single-
rater pilot audits on the development disease before the freeze, found 6.0% (3/50)
and 6.3% (3/48) of claims unsupported, above the 2% target, and the gatekeeper
flagged only 2 of 6 and 1 of 4 of the claims the rater rejected. In six registered
diseases, 350 papers gave 2,836 schema-valid GA4GH Phenopackets; all 241,730
asserted values carry a verbatim quote, which holds by construction and was con-
firmed by a span check. The gatekeeper judged 0.43% (248/57,819) of claims
unsupported in the five confirmatory diseases: a screen of measured low sensi-
tivity, not an error rate. HGVS recall against ClinVar is 10.1%–35.0% at tier 1,
causes untriaged; 57.3% (43/75) of OMIM’s allelic variants are recovered; 214
gap candidates in the registered six (269 across all twelve diseases studied) await
adjudication, and none is yet shown to be a real gap. The pending 250-claim hu-

INTRODUCTION
Rare diseases together affect an estimated 3.5–5.9% of the population (Wakap et al., 2019) and
diagnosis in Europe takes close to five years on average (Faye et al., 2024); a diagnosis often depends
on finding evidence that already exists. That evidence is fragmented across ClinVar (Landrum et al.,
2025), OMIM (Amberger et al., 2019), the Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO) (Gargano et al.,
2024), Mondo (Vasilevsky et al., 2025) and Monarch’s phenopacket-store (Danis et al., 2025), while
most individual patients are described only in case reports. A laboratory querying ClinVar cannot
see a causative variant that was published but never submitted: in one curated gene, 24% of the
pathogenic variants found in PubMed articles were absent from every database examined (Lyons
et al., 2023), and a full-text study reports more than 60,000 likely disease-causing HGMD variants
absent from ClinVar (Birgmeier et al., 2020). Non-standard variant names hide evidence (Freeman
et al., 2024; Lansdon et al., 2026), and phenotype detail in first reports is often superficial (AlMail
et al., 2024).

Alexander disease shows the size of the gap: a 2024 systematic review reports 550 patients and
182 causative GFAP variants (Grossi et al., 2024), against 76 pathogenic or likely pathogenic (P/LP)
single-gene GFAP records in ClinVar’s release of 2026-10-12 and 13 allelic variants in OMIM’s gene
entry (frozen 2026-10-28). None of the twelve genes studied here has a phenopacket-store folder
(commit ca133e6, 2026-11-04). LLM evaluation in rare disease has mostly targeted diagnosis,
where LLMs trail a traditional decision-support tool on phenopacket-derived cases (Reese et al.,
2026) and leakage puts most studies at high risk of bias (Nguyen et al., 2026); building case-level
records with an error rate measured by people has received less attention.
GP-RareLink asks whether SLIDERS (Joshi et al., 2026), a systematic-review method that builds
evidence tables with verbatim excerpts as provenance and reconciles them with a code-writing agent,
can, run with Claude, build such records at an accuracy we can demonstrate. Every extracted value
carries a verbatim quote and the PMID; phenotypes are three-state and “not reported” is never col-
lapsed into “excluded”; a second Claude read, the gatekeeper, removes values their quote does not
support; the output is GA4GH Phenopackets v2. ClinVar and OMIM are never read by extraction,
so that afterwards they can score it (O2) and expose variants absent from both (gap candidates, O3).
People decide what counts: an audit of values against their quotes (O1, primary: fewer than 2% un-
supported in 250 claims), a triage of ClinVar misses and adjudication of gap candidates; nothing goes
to a database without the panel’s approval and the PI’s sign-off. The registered study is six diseases
(Table 2); its pooled headline figures exclude HA20, the development disease. Five reserve diseases
run under a separately pre-registered extension, one exploratory disease and a corpus addendum of
free copies found after the freeze are reported apart and never pooled with it (Section 4.7).
What this report can and cannot claim.
This is version 4 of an internal interim report, written
before any person has rated output of the frozen pipeline; every human result is a boxed placeholder
that a script fills (Table 1). It is a report on provenance and feasibility. The only accuracy evidence
from people is two pre-freeze single-rater pilot audits, both above target (Section 4.2). We contribute
(i) a provenance-first pipeline from case reports to validated phenopackets, run frozen on the regis-
tered six (350 papers, 2,836 phenopackets) and, apart, on six more diseases; (ii) a database-held-out
evaluation whose human parts (audit sample, triage sample, 214 gap candidates in the registered six
with evidence dossiers) are drawn and waiting for people; and (iii) an account of what is not yet
known, stated so that the pending results can settle it.

RELATED WORK
Variant and phenotype extraction.
tmVar 3.0 normalizes variant mentions (Wei et al., 2022) and
LitVar 2.0 indexes about 15 million variants against papers (Allot et al., 2023); its ClinVar links are
kept out of our extraction. AVADA recovered 59% of HGMD’s disease-causing variants from full
text (Birgmeier et al., 2020); PubMind finds about 10% of its mapped variants in ClinVar (Wang &
Wang, 2025); and when GPT-4o, Claude Opus 4 and DeepSeek-V3 extracted disease genes from full
text, disease and phenotype fields were error-prone for all three (Yin et al., 2025). One result bears
on our proband counting and is unfavorable for Claude: among five models counting probands for
PS4, Claude Sonnet 4 had the lowest exact-count concordance (73.0%, against 91.1% for the best)
and the lowest stability (Murugan et al., 2026). For HPO, RAG-HPO reached a best F1 of 0.78 on
112 case reports (Garcia et al., 2025); GPT recognition was non-deterministic (Groza et al., 2024).
The 97.6% HPO precision for Claude Sonnet 4.5 quoted in the project’s application (Larsen et al.,
2026) comes from four synthetic clinical notes in a tool-augmented condition (80.7% without tools),
with recall 65.3%, and Claude was not top on precision or F1. GP-RareLink is compared with no
tagger; its contribution is the quoted, three-state per-person record and its held-out evaluation.
Evidence synthesis and claim verification.
LLM extraction for evidence synthesis has been re-
viewed systematically (Shankar et al., 2026): numeric fields fare worse than text (Kataoka et al.,
2025), reproducibility fell from 94.1% to 77.2% when information was not reported (Jensen et al.,
2025), and otto-SR reached 93.1% extraction accuracy against 79.7% for humans (Cao et al., 2025).
SLIDERS comes from the Lam lab, whose WikiChat grounds and verifies claims (Semnani et al.,
2023).
AIS defines support by a cited span (Rashkin et al., 2023), the gatekeeper’s criterion;
FActScore checks atomic claims (Min et al., 2023), our audit unit; self-verification of cited spans im-
proved clinical extraction (Gero et al., 2023); and clinical fact-checkers have been validated against
clinician labels (Chung et al., 2026). But ungrounded self-correction fails (Huang et al., 2024) and
LLM judges favor their own generations (Zheng et al., 2023; Panickssery et al., 2024). The appli-
cation’s “DRBench” and AA-Omniscience figures are corrected in Appendix I (Rao et al., 2026; Du
et al., 2025; Jackson et al., 2025).
Audits, reference databases and memorization.
Rare-error audits need score or exact intervals
(Brown et al., 2001), and small numerators bound what a sample can certify (Hanley et al., 1983).
A fair open-weight comparison fixes the scaffold (Dorfner et al., 2024). Reference databases are
imperfect ground truth: literature-only ClinVar entries were 8.8% of submissions but 31.4% of
outlier classifications (Yang et al., 2017), ClinVar pathogenic calls have been re-examined (Wright
et al., 2021), and disease annotations are incomplete (Connelly et al., 2026); database overlap with
a method’s inputs inflates accuracy (Grimm et al., 2015). Literature cohorts are phenotype-first
(Wright et al., 2024) and reuse patients (Shivakumar et al., 2026). Membership tests need log-
probabilities unavailable for Claude (Shi et al., 2024) and confound date shift (Duan et al., 2024), so
we use black-box guided completion (Golchin & Surdeanu, 2024) with training cutoffs as a natural
experiment (Roberts et al., 2024).

METHODOLOGY
Diseases, corpus and access.
The registered study is the six diseases of the frozen pre-registration
(Elhani et al., 2024; Didiasova et al., 2020; Koseki et al., 2021; Almarza Novoa et al., 2018; Bram-
batti et al., 2019). Five reserve diseases (Wouters et al., 2014; Vitali et al., 2022; Stockler-Ipsiroglu
et al., 2014; Beyens et al., 2018; Hajialigol et al., 2022) were run under an extension addendum
committed before their extraction; Majeed syndrome (Ferguson et al., 2021) failed that addendum’s
floor (10 eligible open reports, under 15), was searched more widely and run on 24 documents as an
exploratory disease. The corpus came from PubMed (inception to 2026-10-18/19 for the registered
six, 2026-11-14 for the extension) with gene, disease names and a case filter, plus backward citation
search through Europe PMC (Rosonovski et al., 2023). No database citation list seeded a corpus ex-
cept HA20’s pilot corpus (92 of 160 papers, tagged; O2 is reported with and without them). Sonnet
helpers screened with logged reasons (full-text inclusion needed a script-checked quote); a PI check
agreed with 14 of 15 inclusions (exact 95% CI 68–100%) and 5 of 5 exclusions (48–100%). Access
is open only: PMC, Europe PMC and Unpaywall copies, no institutional access, no pages behind
a bot check; supplements capped at 60,000 characters per file. Every registered disease cleared the
substitution floor of 15 eligible reports; natural-history comparator papers are held out.
Extraction.
SLIDERS (Joshi et al., 2026) is run as a home-built version by Claude Code helper
agents on the funded claude.ai account, not the public code. One Claude Opus helper per paper reads
only its paper, the instructions, the schema and the rules, with no web access and an instruction to
use nothing it remembers about the paper (run provenance in the Reproducibility Statement). Four
files were pinned at the freeze; per disease a script swaps only the disease name, gene, schema
path and a 20-item checklist. Every value carries the PMID and a verbatim quote, confirmed by a
deterministic span check; “not reported” carries no quote, and a value without a quote is not emitted,
so complete quote coverage is a property of the pipeline rather than a finding. Normal findings are
recorded as the excluded abnormality, variant fields come only from statements about this person,
and stacked multi-patient cells are not read by position. One gap was frozen on purpose: extraction
has no rule for hedged wording, so hedged statuses are removed by the gatekeeper and end as “not
reported”.
Gatekeeper.
Gatekeeper v4 is a separate Claude Opus read, one helper per chunk of up to 350
claims. It asks whether the quote entails the value for the person the row is about (Rashkin et al.,
2023), records whom the quote is about (this person, a named group, the cohort, another person, the
paper, unclear) and returns supported, partially supported, unsupported or span not found; no value is
exempted by script matching, and phenopackets keep only supported claims. “Unsupported” below
counts unsupported and span not found; “not fully supported” adds partially supported. Because a

model judging its own family’s output can be biased (Zheng et al., 2023), the gatekeeper’s share is a
screen evaluated against human labels, never an error rate.
Records.
People are merged only on an explicit one-to-one citation to an earlier report (a sex-
and-onset route made all 6 false merges of 93 in the pilot and was dropped). VariantValidator 4.0.1
(Freeman et al., 2018) normalizes variants to HGVS (Hart et al., 2024) on MANE Select (Morales
et al., 2022), keeping the paper’s original string; only its normalization fields enter the pipeline.
Where a gene has alternative coding exons (LAMP2, GFAP), the paper’s own exon label chooses
the transcript, else no genomic key is given (a post-freeze change); for Blau, a reference mismatch
on MANE triggers one retry on legacy NM_022162.3. Features are bound to HPO (2026-09-01) and
diseases to Mondo (2026-10-06) by name, never by OMIM cross-reference, and one Phenopacket v2
(Jacobsen et al., 2022) per identity is validated (Danis et al., 2023). Post-freeze changes are logged
deviations (Appendix E).
Held-out ground truth and outcomes.
An automated test fails if any reference path or the tokens
“clinvar”, “omim” or “harness” appear in the extraction code; snapshots live outside the repository,
and database citation seeds, annotation files, LitVar’s ClinVar links, database fields in VariantVal-
idator responses and comparator papers are kept out. References: ClinVar release 2026-10-12 (read
2026-10-19) for the registered six and of 09 Nov 2026 for the rest; OMIM’s entry pages frozen
by the PI on 2026-10-28 (clinical synopses not frozen); HPO annotations on the harness side only
(Appendix A). O1: 250 claims sampled from all gatekeeper-supported claims, stratified by disease,
seeded, drawn before any concordance; raters never see the gatekeeper’s verdict, and an unverifiable
value counts as unsupported. O1 thus measures error in the released records, after the screen; claims
the gatekeeper removed are outside it. Rule C passes if at most 4 are unsupported; P(pass) is 0.99,
0.89, 0.68, 0.44 and 0.13 at true rates of 0.5%, 1%, 1.5%, 2% and 3%, and with 0, 1 or 2 unsup-
ported the exact two-sided 95% upper bound is 1.46%, 2.21% or 2.86%, so a pass does not prove a
rate below 2%. O2: tier 1 = same GRCh38 allele, tier 2 = same protein change for papers with no
cDNA; HGVS recall = share of ClinVar’s (PMID, variant) pairs for extracted PMIDs that the run ex-
tracted from that PMID, so recall is computed only on papers with open full text; overlap = share of
extracted tier-1 alleles in ClinVar (not precision); no threshold. O3: a gap candidate is a gatekeeper-
supported tier-1 allele absent from ClinVar and (registered six) from OMIM; it is a candidate, not a
finding, until adjudicated; submissions will use ClinVar’s curation method. O4: literature frequen-
cies against held-out cohorts with a publication-cluster bootstrap (Efron, 1979) (2,000 resamples,
seed 7425; records with a disease term, comparator members removed), plus a memorization probe.
O5: expert minutes per accepted record, and Claude against an open-weight model under an identi-
cal scaffold. Intervals: Wilson (Wilson, 1927) for proportions, exact Clopper–Pearson (Clopper &
Pearson, 1934) for audit and gatekeeper shares.
Human assessment.
Since 2026-11-15 the audit is done by volunteer raters in rotating groups
after a calibration round with a floor, replacing two fixed raters and a tie-breaker (design in Ap-
pendix F). Nine raters, none identified, rate 40 calibration claims that the PI labelled (20 unsup-
ported, 20 supported, chosen by a rule fixed before any label and amended twice before any rater
saw anything); only a rater who catches at least 15 of the 20 unsupported and passes at least 18 of
the 20 supported serves as a registered rater. Each audit claim is read by 3 raters, with a registered
pair and tie-breaker per claim and rule C unchanged; Cohen’s κ (Cohen, 1960) is reported per pair.
Misses are triaged and candidates adjudicated under ACMG/AMP (Richards et al., 2015) by 2 raters
each, a third where they differ. The PI then chooses Plan A (registered six: 250 claims, 50 misses,
214 candidates) or Plan B (all twelve: 527, 146, 269).

ACCURACY EVIDENCE SO FAR: TWO PILOT AUDITS, AND A SCREEN
Human audits (pilot, pre-freeze).
The only human ratings of extracted claims are two pilot audits
on HA20 by one rater: 6.0% (3/50; exact 95% CI 1.3%–16.5%) and 6.3% (3/48; 1.3%–17.2%)
unsupported, both above the pilot’s own 4% gate. Each exposed an error class (variant fields taken
from a group statement; ages read by position from stacked cells) for which a rule was frozen;
variant fields were 0 of 20 unsupported in the re-audit against 3 of 20 before, but the frozen pipeline
has not been re-audited. Without the 3 claims the rater could not verify, the re-audit found 0 of 45.
Both audits used earlier pipeline versions, and labels in the second-look tab are not independent of
the gatekeeper’s reasons.

O2: CONCORDANCE WITH CLINVAR
The extracted gene matches the disease gene in 97.9%–100% of registered variant rows. HGVS
recall (Figure 3) is low, 10.1%–35.0% at tier 1 and 14.6%–66.7% with protein matches, over ClinVar

5
DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS
Measured: complete provenance (by construction, and checked), valid three-state records from 350
papers, and held-out concordance and gap candidates. Not yet known, and to be decided by the
pending results: whether released values meet the 2% target (O1:
pending: o1_unsupported_pct ); how
much of the low recall is extraction failure rather than citation, conversion or numbering (triage);
which of the 214 registered candidates are true gaps (adjudication); and the bias deviations. The
only human evidence, the pilot audits, sits an order of magnitude above the gatekeeper’s shares, so
those shares are not read as accuracy.
Limitations that change interpretation.
(1) No human-rated accuracy exists for the frozen
pipeline; both pilot audits missed 2% (one rater, HA20, earlier versions), their error classes are
guarded against but not re-audited, and even a pass under rule C leaves a true rate near 2% unex-
cluded. (2) The gatekeeper is a same-family judge with measured low sensitivity (2 of 6, 1 of 4)
and may favor Claude; its procedure was uneven in later runs (scripted verdict files, three addendum
chunks judged once, one sentence judged both ways, softened verdicts, Majeed chunks changed on
re-judging). (3) O1 samples only gatekeeper-supported claims, so it measures released records, not
extraction before the screen. (4) Calibration rests on one person’s reference labels, with a set rule
amended twice after they were seen (before any rater saw anything), and the set is balanced 20/20,
unlike the audit pool, so calibration catch rates do not carry over to audit prevalence. (5) Frozen
extraction gaps remain (no hedge rule; relatives’ variant fields from the proband’s sentence; cohort
statements applied to individuals) and drive higher addendum rates (batch 1 Tangier 4.8%, batch 2
HA20 3.2%). (6) HGVS recall is low and untriaged, and overlap is not precision. (7) Open access
only (14%–53% per registered corpus), so the gap count is a lower bound on a non-random subset;
111 addendum papers and Majeed’s founding reports remain unretrieved. (8) Deduplication is the
weak point: besides the figures above, the pilot made 6 false merges of 93, the registered 50-merge
hand check is not done, and addendum records are unmatched. (9) HA20 is the development disease
(rules tuned on it; 92 of 160 papers ClinVar-seeded), so its figures flatter the pipeline. (10) The
open-weight comparison has no human audit.
Further limitations.
About 10 registered papers had conversion problems and addendum PDFs
OCR damage (helper-reported, unverified); the candidate script has no on-gene check; OMIM was
not checked for the extension or Majeed; the bias arm has no numbers, no comparator for LAD-I and
Tangier, 3 paywalled comparators and many SSADH individuals probably in the comparator reg-
istry. Procedural loose ends: no licence is settled for public outputs that carry verbatim quotes;
unredacted open-weight transcripts remain in the 2026-10-21 to 2026-11-10 history unless it is
rewritten; registered manifests record only the alias “opus”, five extension runs lack a manifest,
the registered gap page still lists the withdrawn Danon candidate, and the pre-registration tags are
not pushed nor the OSF registration made; costs are session estimates. Claude Code built and ran
the pipeline and drafted this report: the numbers are reproducible, the framing is not independent.
