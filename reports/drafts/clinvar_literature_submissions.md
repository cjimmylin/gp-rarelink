**Subject:** Question about literature-only submissions from a curation project (Rare Genomics Institute)

Dear ClinVar team,

The Rare Genomics Institute, with Stanford's OVAL lab and funded by Anthropic's AI for Science program, is running a six-month literature-curation project on six rare diseases. We extract variants and phenotypes from published case reports, each with its PMID and a verbatim quote. We compare them with ClinVar, and have the variants absent from ClinVar reviewed by a panel of three PhD scientists before offering them as submissions.

In our pilot on A20 haploinsufficiency (*TNFAIP3*), 55 of 95 normalized variants extracted from 85 open-access papers had no ClinVar record in the 2026-10-12 release. For 41 of those, the protein change the paper states matches the normalized cDNA. These are candidates only; none has been reviewed by our panel yet.

Before we prepare any submission, could you advise us on:
1. the right collection method and submitter type for variants we did not observe ourselves but curated from published reports ("literature only");
2. whether the classification should be our panel's own, with stated assertion criteria, or a record of the authors' classification;
3. what evidence you want for each submission (citations, phenotype as HPO terms, zygosity, inheritance), and the preferred format (submission spreadsheet or API);
4. how to handle a variant reported in several papers, or a patient reported more than once;
5. whether a research organization needs to register as a submitter first, and how long that takes.

We expect to prepare a small number of submissions after our panel's review in early 2027 [fill: confirm timing], and would rather get the format right before we start.
